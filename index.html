01 · About the workshop

The close-up view responders need.

When a monsoon flood moves through the Indus basin, responders need answers fast: which embankments breached, which bridges failed, how many homes are lost?

Drones can reach a disaster site within hours and record it in centimetre detail, giving responders a close-up view that wide-area imagery cannot.

This workshop takes participants from the basics of UAVs and remote sensing to planning a survey, flying it and turning the images into maps and 3D models, using standard commercial UAVs and established processing software. Everything demonstrated can be reproduced with equipment ordinarily available to a Pakistani institution.

By the close of Day 3, every participant will have produced:

  1. Flight planfor a real survey mission
  2. Drone imageryorthophoto, oblique and thermal
  3. Orthophoto mapfrom a survey they helped fly
  4. 3D modelfrom oblique photography
  5. Response planfrom the capstone exercise

03 · Programme

From the wide view to the close-up.

Doors open at 9:00 AM and sessions start at 9:15 AM each day. Days 1 and 2 end at 4:00 PM; on Day 3 sessions end at 12:15 PM and the closing ceremony follows Friday prayers at 2:30 PM. Pick a day, then open any session for its content.

  1. 01 Wed · 18 Nov

    UAV and remote sensing basics

    1. 09:00–09:15Registration · badge collection and laptop check
    2. 09:15–09:35Opening ceremony

      Welcome address and inauguration of the workshop.

    3. 09:35–10:35Module 1 · Introduction to UAVs

      Definition, development and characteristics of UAVs; composition and basic principles of UAV systems; related industries, norms and regulations; administrative departments and general operating procedures in Pakistan; requirements for remote sensing platforms and how to choose one by model, flight time, altitude, speed, payload capacity and integration.

    4. 10:35–10:50Tea break
    5. 10:50–11:50Module 2 · Remote sensing basics

      Remote sensing types, definitions and scope; fundamentals of photogrammetry; remote sensing applications; software demonstration and practical operation.

    6. 11:50–12:30Module 3 · UAV remote sensing in disaster management

      Disaster risk assessment, monitoring and early warning, emergency response, and loss assessment and recovery monitoring, through practical cases. How UAV remote sensing compares with traditional methods in response speed, resolution, flexibility and cost.

    7. 12:30–13:45Lunch and prayers
    8. 13:45–14:45Module 4 · Sensors and data acquisition

      Basic principles of UAV data acquisition; working principles and data characteristics of optical, multispectral, thermal and LiDAR sensors; geometric correction and radiometric calibration; how to obtain high-quality, usable data.

    9. 14:45–15:00Tea break
    10. 15:00–15:45Flight planning exercise

      Principles and methods of UAV flight planning: delineating the flight area and avoiding prohibited and restricted zones; setting altitude, speed and image overlap for the sensor and terrain; automatic route generation and manual adjustment. Groups plan the mission they will fly on Day 2.

    11. 15:45–16:00Wrap-up and Q&A

      Recap of the day and a preview of Day 2.

  2. 02 Thu · 19 Nov

    UAV data processing and practice

    1. 09:00–09:15Arrival and tea
    2. 09:15–10:30Module 5 · UAV data processing

      Data products and the principles and basic workflow of UAV data processing; position and attitude (POS) data organization; image preprocessing, quick stitching, registration and correction; output of results; oblique photography, its equipment and applications; mainstream 3D processing software and the oblique workflow from checking and import to adjustment, modelling and output.

    3. 10:30–10:45Transfer to the NUST field site · near the Saddle Club
    4. 10:45–12:30Practical 1 · Mission flights

      Basic flight operations; emergency take-off and landing, including on uneven ground; one-click return-to-home settings; flying the planned mission route; multi-source data collection: orthophoto, oblique photography and thermal infrared images.

    5. 12:30–13:45Return to IGIS, lunch and prayers
    6. 13:45–15:15Practical 2 · Processing software

      Hands-on with UAV processing software on IGIS workstations: data preprocessing, orthophoto generation and oblique photography 3D modelling.

    7. 15:15–15:30Tea break
    8. 15:30–16:00Wrap-up and Q&A

      Recap of the day and a preview of Day 3.

    The processing practical uses a prepared dataset so it fits the time; the same dataset is the backup if weather or airspace prevents flying.

  3. 03 Fri · 20 Nov

    Integration, case studies & rapid response

    1. 09:00–09:15Arrival and tea
    2. 09:15–09:45Module 6 · Remote sensing in disaster response

      How remote sensing supports a real emergency response: deciding where to look and where to fly, getting permissions quickly, turning imagery into products responders can use, and who does what, and how quickly.

    3. 09:45–10:20Applied case studies

      Three Pakistani studies:

      • Flood risk assessment of Attabad Lake using a scenario-based approach.
      • Shisper Lake glacial lake outburst flood scenarios with HEC-RAS 2D.
      • Riverine flooding in Jhang district and the summer monsoon over the Upper Indus Basin.
    4. 10:20–10:30Short break · scenario packs handed out
    5. 10:30–11:30Capstone · Rapid-response simulation

      Groups take a disaster scenario and prepare a short remote sensing response plan: what to survey and why, which data and sensors to use, how to collect them, and the products they would deliver to responders. Scenarios include monsoon flooding in Dera Ghazi Khan, a glacial lake outburst in Hunza, urban flooding in Karachi and post-flood recovery in Sindh.

    6. 11:30–12:15Group presentations

      Each group presents its brief, with feedback from the instructors.

    7. 12:15–14:30Friday prayers and lunch
    8. 14:30–15:30Closing ceremony

      Course summary, remarks by dignitaries, award of certificates and group photograph.

04 · Learning outcomes

What you’ll be able to do.

  1. Explain the composition, principles and types of UAV systems, and choose a platform for a remote sensing task.
  2. State Pakistan’s UAV regulations and operating procedures, and follow correct practice for permission and flight safety.
  3. Describe remote sensing types and the fundamentals of photogrammetry.
  4. Explain how UAV remote sensing supports each stage of disaster management, and its advantages over traditional methods.
  5. Describe how optical, multispectral, thermal and LiDAR sensors work and how to obtain high-quality data.
  6. Plan a UAV survey: flight area, altitude, speed, overlap and route.
  7. Take part in a supervised mission flight and collect orthophoto, oblique and thermal imagery.
  8. Process UAV imagery into an orthophoto and an oblique 3D model.
  9. Prepare a remote sensing response plan for a disaster scenario.

International and national expertise in remote sensing and disaster risk.

    Professor · Institute of Mountain Hazards and Environment (IMHE), CAS

Works on glacier and glacial lake hazards and new remote sensing methods; led the Glacier and Glacial Lake Monitoring Network and contributed to disaster risk assessment for the China–Pakistan Economic Corridor. More than 50 papers, including ESI highly cited work; review expert for NSFC, MOST, ERC and NERC.

  • Engr. WANG Wen

    Assistant Engineer · Mountain Science Data Center, IMHE, CAS

    Specializes in multi-source remote sensing for mountain hazards and the cryosphere, including glacial lake inventories and freeze–thaw disaster monitoring. Extensive UAV field experience, from flight planning and image quality control to orthomosaics, DEMs, point clouds and 3D models. Has contributed to national and provincial projects, papers and patents on glacial lake extraction, and teaches with case studies from High Asia.

  • Dr. Javed Iqbal

    Professor · SCEE – IGIS, NUST

    Over three decades of teaching, research and leadership; PhD from Mississippi State University and MS from the University of Arkansas. Works on soil science, GIS and remote sensing, and hazard mapping of landslides, glacier avalanches and flash floods. More than 200 scholarly contributions (h-index 29); supervised over 100 MS and 4 PhD students; funding from DAAD, NSF and Erasmus.

  • Dr. Shahid Nawaz Khan

    Assistant Professor · SCEE – IGIS, NUST

    PhD in Remote Sensing (University of Alabama) and MS in GIScience (South Dakota State University); President’s and Rector’s Gold Medals at NUST. Researches explainable, spatially explicit machine learning for Earth observation, SAR–optical fusion, large-scale Earth Engine workflows and UAV remote sensing. FAA Part 107-certified remote pilot with 200+ flight hours.

06 · Before you arrive

Come ready to work.

The schedule is compressed, so please have these ready before Day 1.

Any ordinary laptop is enough. Day 2 processing runs on IGIS workstations, and you take home a sample dataset to repeat the workflow later.

  • A laptop for the software demonstrations and exercises.
  • QGIS 3.34 LTR installed, with at least 5 GB of free disk space.
  • A UAV ground-station app on your phone or tablet for mission planning, charged, with a cable.
  • Field clothing for Day 2 morning: closed-toe shoes, sun protection and a hat. The site is open ground with little shade.
  • The pre-reading pack, circulated in advance: sensor terminology, correction concepts and the disaster management cycle.
  • Signed field-safety acknowledgement, handed in at registration.
