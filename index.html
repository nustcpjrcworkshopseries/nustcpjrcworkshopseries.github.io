01 — About the course

From the sky to the situation room.

To enable personnel to master UAV remote sensing data collection, processing and analysis — strengthening their ability to support disaster management decisions with efficient, accurate technical evidence.

The second edition of the NUST × CPJRC workshop series builds on the 2025 course with the same blend of classroom theory, field flights and processing labs, delivered by researchers from the Aerospace Information Research Institute of the Chinese Academy of Sciences and faculty from NUST.

Who should attend

  • Disaster management professionalsEmergency services, planning and relief agencies
  • ResearchersGeospatial, hydrology, hazards and environment
  • FacultyTeaching remote sensing, GIS or civil engineering

02 — Programme

Four days, one complete workflow.

Each day runs in two sessions: 9:00 AM – 12:00 PM and 2:00 PM – 5:00 PM. Open a session to see the full syllabus.

  1. 01Day one

    Inauguration, fundamentals & disaster scenarios

    Registration and opening ceremony

    Welcome address and inauguration of the workshop.

    Basics of UAVs and remote sensing principles

    Introduction to UAVs — definition, development and characteristics; norms, regulations, administrative departments and general operating procedures; platform requirements for remote sensing; choosing an aircraft (model, flight time, altitude, speed, payload and integration).

    Remote sensing basics — foundations of photogrammetry, applications, and designing and evaluating flight plans (survey area, sensor type, overlap, resolution, efficiency, accuracy and safety).

    UAV remote sensing in disaster management

    Risk assessment — how terrain and land use shape susceptibility. Monitoring and early warning — tracking precursors of landslides, debris flows, floods and earthquakes. Emergency response — rapid information gathering and rescue route planning. Loss assessment and recovery — counting damage to houses, infrastructure and crops to quantify losses.

  2. 02Day two

    Data acquisition, processing theory & modelling

    Principles and flight planning for data acquisition

    Basic principles of data acquisition; imaging principles of optical, spectral and LiDAR sensors; pre-processing (geometric correction and radiometric calibration); flight planning — delineating areas, setting altitude and speed, and route planning.

    Data processing for UAV applications

    Position and attitude (POS) data processing, rectification and 3D processing, and oblique photogrammetry: POS organisation, image pre-processing, quick stitching, registration and correction, and the oblique workflow from checking and import through adjustment, modelling and output.

  3. 03Day three

    Hands-on flights & disaster case studies

    Practical operations
    • Mission flights — basic operation, emergency take-off and landing, mission route planning.
    • Multi-source data collection — orthophotos, oblique photography and thermal infrared.
    • Processing and analysis — orthophoto and oblique workflows, rapid loss assessment through change detection and 3D modelling (e.g. landslide displacement).
    Applications and case studies
    • Flood risk assessment of Attabad Lake with a scenario-based approach to preparedness.
    • Scenario-based HEC-RAS 2D unsteady flow analysis of Shisper Lake for GLOF risk.
    • Riverine flooding in Jhang district, linked to ENSO and 2010 monsoon rainfall over the Upper Indus Basin.
  4. 04Day four

    Review, exchange & closing ceremony

    Open session and Q&A

    Exchange among participants and lecturers; questions and answers on the full workflow.

    Closing ceremony

    Course summary, remarks by dignitaries and award of certificates to participants.
